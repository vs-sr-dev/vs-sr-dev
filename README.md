# Silicon Relics

Homebrew, ports and preservation research for **obscure and failed vintage platforms** — the machines that flopped, got cancelled, or were never documented, and so have almost no public technical record.

The goal is concrete: take technical knowledge that exists nowhere else — compiler bugs, hardware register maps, undocumented file formats, emulator internals — and make it **publicly verifiable** for the people who come after. For several of these platforms, these repositories are the only public documentation of their kind.

Everything below is either original code or documentation. **No repository here contains game assets, ROM data or copyrighted content** — the tools read media you already own.

*Everything here is published as is. This is reverse-engineering work, not a specification: findings are revisited and corrected over time, but nothing in these repositories is guaranteed to be complete or correct, whether or not a given page says so. Where a claim can be checked mechanically, the repository ships the command that checks it — run that rather than trusting the prose.*

On ARM the two cursors are **unrepresentable as immediates** and live in the literal pool while 4080 does not, so the scan becomes two passes with the literal hits cross-referenced against every PC-relative load; a module has to be **decompressed before it is scanned**, because a DS `arm9.bin` is normally `BLZ`-packed and a scan of one returns a clean-looking zero; every THUMB hit has to be checked against the ARM word containing it, since a THUMB-only probe invented **24 of 24** false `add #19` out of ARM code; the innocent 4080 hits should be expected to be **trigonometry**; a blind decode has to sweep **per member and then the complement**, because the plausibility bound does nothing inside a 134 MB buffer; and of the platform's seven compression formats only **`LZ77`** can be ruled out by decoding at all, the rest accepting arbitrary data. The twelfth build is that control, and it is the first here opened to test another build rather than itself. *Tales of Innocence* (**Nintendo DS**, 2007) holds the publisher, the platform and the series and changes the developer again — and it returns the same zero, cleaner: **not one of the five constants in either ARM encoding** across five modules, 78,489 immediates and 554,020 words, zero refills, zero 4,096-byte rings, twelve wrappers with **zero callers over 40,411 branch targets**, and **zero blocks in 23,083 payloads and 657,419,133 bytes**. And then it **compresses** — 102 files as BIOS `LZ77`, 16.9 MB becoming 32.1 MB, in 1,344 archives of its own design, with CRI and Actimagine middleware, 51.4% of the cartridge media and **3.2% of it empty**. So **the Nintendo DS is not where the codec stops**, and the 2006 cartridge's raw data is a fact about the 2006 cartridge. What survives is the codebase — and the corpus now states why that is weaker than it looks: **both DS developers are outside the line**, so two zeros from two outsiders are as consistent with *the codebase never shipped a DS title* as with *the boundary is the codebase*, and the open question is limited by what was made rather than by what was measured. That build also forced the checklist's remaining corrections, three of them defects rather than additions: the DS **difference-filter type bytes are `0x81` and `0x82`**, not `0x80` and `0x81` — `0x80` is not a stream type, and 2,444 CRI audio files begin `0x80 0x00`; the BIOS **Huffman walk** needs the leaf mask `0x80 >> bit` and a child address taken from the node's own address, because the tree starts at an odd one, and both errors read as *tree overrun*, i.e. as a clean negative; **"the BIOS format" and "the BIOS service" are separate findings** and a build can have one without the other; and a per-file pass **fails silently on a directory tree and on a container**, in the direction of a clean-looking negative. The corrections came from the cartridge itself — it ships one animation **five times over, once per format**, beside the original, which is the first positive control this corpus has ever had for those five decoders. The thirteenth build is the one the twelfth asked for, and it is not on that machine at all. *Tales of Symphonia: Ratatosk no Kishi* (**Wii**, 2008) is the direct sequel to the 2003 GameCube build, from **inside the line**, on the **same processor family** — so the strong byte test finally runs across a change of console and returns **10 bytes** of shared decoder against **835 contiguous bytes** of shared `OSSaveFPUContext` that neither of two unrelated Wii controls has, with **96 shared regions and 12,143 bytes** in total and not one of them touching either decoder copy. So *the codebase never shipped a Nintendo title* is **false by measurement** — it shipped two, and the first carries the codec four times — and for the first time the boundary is the *Tales* console codebase with nothing standing beside it. What is left is a **date**: the codec's last confirmed appearance is *Abyss*, 25 November 2005, and its first confirmed absence from the line is June 2008. And that build separates the two halves cleanly, because **the packer crossed where the decoder did not**: the compressed stream behind both discs' fake Microsoft Cabinet header carries `5b 80 80 8d` at offset `+8` in **2,051 of 2,051 payloads**, 545 written in 2003 and 1,506 in 2008, with the four bytes in front uniformly random in both. Its own corrections to the checklist are about instruments: **a negative control has to be in the same instruction set**, so `VENUS.ELF` cannot be quoted against a PowerPC needle; when no shared library routine is known, the runtime control can be **found** by taking the longest run two builds share and subtracting what a stranger also has, which turns a worthless 1,275 into a meaningful 835; on a four-gigabyte medium the **denominators invert**, a four-byte needle having a chance rate of 1.00 where a 128 MB cartridge gives 0.031, so a single hit means nothing, a zero is weak and every hit has to be read — 3,865 of them on that disc, all inside the word `vector_`; and a blind decode must descend into **media containers too**, because a 495 MB `THP` taken as one buffer sweeps at 156 KB/s and never finishes. The fourteenth build then takes the date away again, from six days on the other side of it. *Tales of Vesperia* (**Xbox 360**, 7 August 2008) has a volume stamped **2008-06-20** and **carries the codec** — four constant sites in one pair of routines out of **3,989,504** PowerPC instruction words with **zero** `4080` to disassemble, both preload loops, the nine-byte header still little-endian on a big-endian machine, the run escape, and **8,255 of 8,255** blocks decoding, 337,852,435 into 775,930,739. So the Wii zero is a fact about *that project*: two builds, one line, one quarter, two consoles, opposite decisions, and **nothing line-wide to date**. It also reverses the packer finding — that disc has **no `MSCF` and no `5b 80 80 8d`** anywhere on 7.84 GB, its container being `FPS4` — so across the three 2003–2008 builds the codec appears twice and the cabinet compressor twice and *they are not the same two*; neither is the constant. And it is the first build where the strong test is **architecturally available and practically is not**: 872 bytes of the 2003 decoder score **10**, but the whole-image control finds a ceiling of **28 bytes** between Metrowerks and Microsoft Visual C++, so the ten has no denominator and the descent has to be read off the constants and the structure, which match *Abyss*'s six times out of six. Its two corrections to the checklist both came from the **negative controls**: the structural probe scored **zero refills on a build containing two**, because MSVC allocates a separate destination where Metrowerks writes in place; and *Eternal Sonata*, run as a control, carries an LZSS with a cursor at **4078**, the `+3` and the same nibble layout — because those are the **canonical 1989 LZSS reference implementation's** constants, not this codebase's. Nothing published here rests on the constant alone, but the shortcut's advertised strength does not survive it: the scan finds *an* LZSS and only the fingerprint cluster — the synthetic preload, the `\| 0xFF00` refill, the nine-byte header, the run escape — says which. ## Reading the repository names

`<platform>-<subject>`, where the platform is the **target**, not the origin. So [`psx-lba`](https://github.com/vs-sr-dev/psx-lba) is Little Big Adventure *running on* PlayStation, and [`pc-mikie`](https://github.com/vs-sr-dev/pc-mikie) is an arcade game *brought to* PC.

Two suffixes narrow it further: **`-doc`** is documentation only, and **`-re`** is a reverse-engineering devlog. Anything without a suffix ships runnable code.

## The wider Silicon Relics project

These repositories are part of a larger, ongoing body of cross-console work — an analog-horror ARG series and the homebrew and reverse-engineering that underpins it, spanning many years and many forgotten machines. Which platforms, and in what order, is something the series leaves for players to discover.

This is where that technical work surfaces publicly first.

## On method and attribution

This is a human + LLM collaboration, stated openly because the honesty is the point. **Claude acts as a technical bridge:** I bring decades of domain knowledge and direction — knowing these platforms, recognising when a result is a genuine first, and deciding where it's worth digging — and Claude provides implementation across architectures I could not otherwise approach: the drivers, the emulator patches, the disassembly and log forensics, the documentation.
